An authentic Thai lettuce wrap recipe that is fast to make, incredibly fresh, fragrant and spicy.

## Ingredients

### Sauce

- 2 tsp cornflour, or 2 tbsp uncooked rice (any rice is fine) (Note 1)
- 3 tbsp water
- 2 1/2 tbsp lime juice (1–2 limes)
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tsp brown sugar

### Larb

- 2 tbsp peanut oil (or another high-smoke-point cooking oil)
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated or very finely chopped
- 2 large garlic cloves, minced
- 1 lemongrass stalk, white and very pale green part only, finely chopped (Note 2)
- 2 Thai or bird's eye chillies, deseeded and finely chopped (adjust to taste)
- 500g chicken mince or pork mince
- 1/2 red onion, cut into 4 wedges then finely sliced
- 1/3 cup coriander leaves, plus extra to garnish
- 1/3 cup mint leaves, plus extra to garnish

### Serving

- 3 tbsp crushed peanuts (optional)
- 6–8 small to medium lettuce leaves (baby cos works well) (Note 3)
- Extra lime wedges and chilli

## Method

1. Sauce: Mix the water with the cornflour or rice powder in a small bowl to make a slurry. Add the lime juice, fish sauce and sugar, mix to combine, then set aside.
2. Heat the peanut oil in a wok or heavy-based frying pan over medium-high heat. Add the ginger, garlic, lemongrass and chilli, then sauté for 45 seconds to 1 minute until fragrant. Do not let the garlic burn or it will taste bitter.
3. Add the chicken and turn the heat up to high. Cook, breaking the mince into small pieces.
4. When the chicken has turned white and is almost cooked through, after about 3–4 minutes, add the sauce. Cook for 45 seconds to 1 minute, until the chicken is coated and the sauce thickens.
5. Remove the wok from the heat. Stir through the onion, coriander and mint.
6. Spoon the filling into a bowl. Serve with the lettuce, peanuts, extra herbs and lime wedges on the side so everyone can make their own. Spoon the filling into lettuce cups and add your preferred garnishes.

## My notes

- Serves 2–3 as a light main.
- Note 1 — For traditional ground rice, dry-toast the rice in a wok or heavy-based frying pan over high heat for 5 minutes, until dark golden brown. Grind it to a powder with a mortar and pestle and use it instead of cornflour.
- Note 2 — Use fresh lemongrass or 1 tbsp lemongrass paste. Add the paste with the chicken. For fresh lemongrass, peel away the tough, stringy outer layers and use the bottom 7–10cm.
- Note 3 — Soft or crisp lettuce both work. Crisp leaves with a natural cup shape are easiest to fill; soft leaves can be bundled into rolls.
- For a non-traditional extra, serve with Thai peanut satay sauce.
